# Content strategy (step 3)

Settled with Jonathan, 2026-07-24.

## What Civic Workbench is

An informal civic tech incubator — a handful of people creating projects
and writing about them. Established 2016. Interests: data science, language
models, social media, open web, open standards, tech harm reduction, and
adjacent topics.

## Audience

Two co-primary readers:

1. **Funders & partners** — foundations, civic orgs, government people
   deciding whether to fund or partner. The site must signal credibility
   and a track record.
2. **Civic tech community** — peers who read the writeups and reports for
   their substance.

The conversion event is small and human: someone reads a report, thinks
"these are serious people," and sends an email.

## Page inventory

Six things, nothing else:

| Page | Contents |
|------|----------|
| **Landing** | Short declarative statement of what CW is (the main place the collective voice speaks); most recent stream entries; compact project index (list with one-line description + status — no project pages). Contact email present. |
| **About** | History of the org since 2016; the interest areas; how the incubator works informally. Depth lives here so the landing can stay short. |
| **Stream** | One chronological feed, two entry types: **writeups/reports** (long-form, the main event) and **project notes** (short updates). Index page + one page per entry. |
| **Team** | The handful of people: name, a couple of sentences, links to their own sites. No headshot-grid corporatism. |
| **Contact** | An email address on landing/team/about — not its own page. |
| **Footer/colophon** | RSS (load-bearing — it IS the subscription model for this audience), how the site is built. |

## Structure decision

**One stream, two types.** No /projects section with per-project pages; the
project index on the landing is a list. Writing is the spine of the site.

## Voice

**Bylined individuals, collective home.** Entries carry individual authors'
names; "we" appears on landing/about only. Register: plain-language,
technically literate, no comms-department gloss — USWDS plain-language
guidance meets an engineer's README. Reports are rigorous without being
academic.

## Editorial model — "slow burn" as a feature

The site must look intentional at ~4 posts a year:

- Dates shown proudly. A 2024 report near the top signals durable work,
  not neglect.
- No "news"/"latest" framing, no empty-state promises ("more soon!").
- No tag taxonomy or archive apparatus that only makes sparseness visible.
- Entries written to still read well in three years.
- Reference model: Ink & Switch — infrequent, heavyweight, worth citing.

## Prototype content plan

Content today is aspirational, so prototypes use invented-but-realistic
material: 2–3 fictional civic-tech projects, one long report excerpt, a
couple of project notes, and team bios. Project names must read as clearly
fictional/placeholder so a shared prototype never passes as a real claim
of work. No lorem ipsum anywhere — prototypes are judged with real-feeling
content or not at all.
